原题：You need to be delusional if you want to succeed
作者：Dan Koe（@thedankoe） | 发布：2026-09-13（北京时间；原始时间戳为 2026-09-12 16:40 UTC） | 原文：https://x.com/thedankoe/status/2098813973714149757
译注：delusional 一词本义为"妄想的、不切实际的"，作者取其褒义，指远超常理的目标，统一译作"妄想式的"。原文"Job's"应为"Jobs's"（乔布斯的）；"who's money"应为"whose money"。小标题编号原文为"Step 1) / 2) / Step 3)"，格式不统一，照录。

告诉我，下面这些是不是在说你：

1）你觉得在"好生活是什么样子"这件事上，你被骗了。

你在追求一个跟奶奶吃饭时说起来很体面的目标。或者一个不会让你父母觉得你很让人失望的目标。而你看不到它会通向哪里。

如果你忽视这种感觉，你会在 10 年后醒来，拥有一个学位、一份还算体面的工作，以及一种永无止境的绝望——你永远没法追逐自己的梦想了。

2）你很有效率，但实际上并没有朝任何东西取得进展。

你早起。你听播客。你写日记、你举铁、你的日程排得满满的，但你不知道自己要去哪里。

再过 5 年，你还会在"想清楚这辈子要做什么"。

3）你有抱负，却保持沉默，因为说出来在朋友和家人面前显得很尴尬。

你想要一种独特的生活。你不想合群。但当人们问起你的未来时，你把它冲淡，免得有人笑话。

你陷入精神自慰。你因为想要与众不同而觉得自己很特别，却仍然做着和其他所有人一样的事。

没人想落到这 3 种处境中的任何一种。

这封信会为你改变这一点。

因为我最喜欢做的事情之一，就是研究成功者和平庸者的行动。而成功其实不过如此。多做那些通向成功的事，少做那些通向平庸的事。接下来的几封信都会专门讲这个。

当我们逆向分析那些做成了不可能之事的人——埃隆·马斯克、史蒂夫·乔布斯，以及过去其他最伟大的创意人、思想家和建造者——我注意到他们之间有一个共同的模式。

他们的目标是妄想式的。

这就是他们成功的关键。

而这正是我们要讨论的——为什么普通的目标导致失败、妄想式目标的心理学、如何进入和超级成功者同一个现实，以及如何实现大多数人认为不可能的事。

为什么"现实的"目标导致失败和平庸（你默认就属于这一类）

聪明工程师最常犯的错误，是去优化一个本不该存在的东西。

——埃隆·马斯克

你有目标，即使你没有设定它们。

心智就是这样运作的。而如果你意识到这一点，你就能很快看出，为什么你设定的任何目标你都坚持不下去。

我们说的不是新年决心，或者其他某个表面化的目标——你因为对镜子里的自己感觉不好而设定它，然后立刻放弃，第二天就忘了（因为另一个无意识的目标——通常是方便和舒适，或者"别引人注意"这个目标——对你来说比终于健身更重要）。

我们说的是，你的每一个念头、每一个行动都在瞄准一个目标。就像一枚热追踪导弹。你在默默地为一个你从未选择、或者别人替你选择的目标做优化。

花一秒钟想想这一点。

你想的或做的每一件事，都朝向一个目标。

这很重要。极其重要。它意味着，如果你不知道那些目标是什么，你就没有掌控自己的人生。

事情是这样发展的：

  • 你出生了
  • 你的第一个目标是生存
  • 你的父母已经想了很久，他们希望你追求什么目标。他们"希望你过得最好"
  • 你的父母教你走路、教你交流，最终教你他们为你设想的成功是什么样子，而那通常是社会传给他们的目标（上学、找份好工作、赞美你的神）
  • 你去上学，然后上大学，因为那就是"下一步"
  • 你找到工作，达成里程碑，在格子里打勾

然后，在度过将近四分之一的人生后，你开始怀疑这到底是不是你最初想要的。

熵说明了系统趋向无序。应用到心智上，精神熵意味着你的心智，以及你的生活，会漂向无序和混乱。

在某个时刻，一个系统会变得不稳定，一个新的、更有序的结构必须涌现，就像一个喷水的水龙头最终会变成一个漩涡。

在你的人生中，你会到达一个时刻：你较低的那部分想保持原样，较高的那部分想要更多。感觉就像被撕成两半。那是一个信号，提示你彻底扭转人生。

现实的目标之所以导致失败和平庸，是因为你选择它们的方式。你看看你认为可能的东西，然后再把它冲淡一点，免得别人笑话你或者觉得你是个白痴。一个目标之所以感觉现实，只是因为它符合你的父母、老师或五个最亲密的朋友原本就预期你会过的那种生活。

换句话说，你的心智在一个笼子里。

而如果你一直选择住在那个笼子里，你就永远没有机会注意到一个可能改变人生的机会，更别说付诸行动了，你的生活会慢慢变糟，直到痛苦大到你要么被压垮，要么无论如何决定去追求一个妄想式的目标。

妄想式目标的心理学

一个把自己设想为"失败型人格"的人，会找到某种方式失败，无论他有多好的意图或意志力，即使机会确确实实被扔到了他腿上。一个把自己设想为不公的受害者、"注定要受苦"的人，总是会找到能验证自己看法的境遇。

——麦克斯韦·马尔茨（Maxwell Maltz）

人类之所以独特，不是因为他们拥有记忆、意识，或者比动物更精细的智能。

他们之所以独特，是因为他们不断地模拟未来来引导现在。

你想未来比想过去更多，而未来是导致你当下行为的原因。而不是反过来。你的行动并不创造未来。你先创造未来，然后朝它行动。

这叫选择性注意。

你的大脑要么被刺激牵着走，要么被一个目标引导。

而当你专注于一件事时，按定义，这意味着你没有专注于你本可以专注的其他 99.99% 的事情。你的注意力是由目标引导的，而不是像一个布娃娃一样被抛来抛去，寻找下一次廉价的多巴胺刺激。

妄想式目标会迫使这一点发生。

想象两个目标截然不同的人。

汤米的目标和几乎所有人一样——上学、受教育、找一份体面的工作，好让他终于负担得起一套不错的公寓，并获得足够的物质地位象征来吸引伴侣。

约翰尼的目标看起来不可能——在 6 个月内建立一个月入 10 万美元的内容生意。

汤米有没有可能赚到比一份"体面工作"更多的钱？汤米会不会在学校之外学到一些让他能追求更赚钱机会的东西？不会，因为他的注意力不是在选择这些。他可能会接触到改变人生的信息，而他的心智会把它当作"噪音"，仅仅因为它对实现他所追求的目标来说不是必需的。

另一方面，约翰尼无法像普通人那样思考。为了实现那个妄想式的目标，他意识到自己正在采取的大多数行动都完全没用。

一个设定得当的妄想式目标会把你的大脑推入未知，加速神经可塑性。一致性会强化你大脑中的通路，但新奇和挑战会更快地刺激学习和改变。拨动开关、全力投入一个目标，会把你的大脑扔进一个它别无选择、只能快速重新布线的环境，而新的行为会成为你新的基线。

这时，心流状态和痴迷开始创造一种幸福的生活质量。5 种内在驱动力开始叠加。

  • 好奇心——探索未知、学习如何改变、填补知识空白的渴望。产生来自新奇的良性多巴胺和去甲肾上腺素，后者提升注意力，让你准备好学习。
  • 热情——对那条能让你改变人生的道路建立起一种强烈的热忱。产生更多良性多巴胺和去甲肾上腺素。
  • 使命——感觉你的行动在为比你自己更大的东西做出贡献。实现目标会带来更多多巴胺，从而强化行为。血清素来自意义感和归属感。催产素来自连接。
  • 自主——主导自己生活和工作的渴望。掌控你的选择、行动和环境。再次带来更多多巴胺，并降低皮质醇（感觉被关进盒子里的压力），从而让创造性的决策成为可能。
  • 精通——学习和成长的过程本身就是奖励。产生可持续的良性多巴胺，让你留在游戏里。

这些听起来都很好，但我能听到你的反对意见。

"可是 Dan，我以前试过设定这样的目标，但这些一样都没发生。"

是的。

你的目标需要引力。它需要强大到让你生活中的其他一切都显得愚蠢而毫无意义。

这很难做到，我会再写一封信讲如何对一个改变人生的目标变得痴迷的种种细节，但现在我们先来学习如何正确地设定一个妄想式目标。

如何进入超级成功者所处的同一个现实

每一个伟大的想法，一开始都是亵渎。

——安东尼·德梅勒（Anthony De Mello）

史蒂夫·乔布斯会走进一个房间，宣布一个不可能的截止日期或一个不可能的产品，仿佛它已经定下来了。

"我们会在 1 月发货，它会做到这个"——带着如此彻底的确信，以至于房间里那些知道这不可能的工程师离开时会相信它，然后朝它建造，好像那只是计划而已。

这就是乔布斯的现实扭曲力场。

大多数人把它视为魅力、操纵，或者他是个混蛋，而它其实只是他在重新瞄准房间里所有人的注意力。

乔布斯拒绝被"这个时间表不可能行得通"这一刺激牵着走，用一个未来的目标覆盖了它。房间里的每一个大脑开始选择一条路径，而不是选择它行不通的理由。

关键就在这里。

你的目标不是一个你要抵达的终点。那是对目标最自我提升式的诠释之一。相反，目标是一个过滤器，决定你的大脑把什么看作重要、忽略什么。目标是一种视角。你是否真的达成了目标无关紧要，因为你至少会落在目标附近，取得你以前从没想过可能的进展。

现实没有义务去符合你偏爱的那套关于事情该如何运作的模型。

能力来自把自己发展到足以理解现实，然后在现实中有效地运作。

（译注：这条回复与本节"现实扭曲力场""目标先于行动创造未来"的说法形成直接张力，可作为对照阅读。）

要正确地设定一个妄想式目标、创造你自己的现实扭曲力场，需要做到以下几点。

拿出一个笔记本，把这些写下来：

第 1 步）装上这个视角。

首先，想一个大到你认为不可能的目标。

然后，设定一个短到你认为不可能的时间线。

如果你不知道该设定什么目标，甚至不知道这辈子该做什么，想一想你人生中当下最痛苦的那个问题——解决它会让其他问题更容易解决——会有帮助。

对大多数人来说，那通常是他们的健康体能或财务。

走进健身房、减掉 20 磅，能为你的自信和精力带来奇效，让你更容易开始扭转人生。

多赚钱能缓解压力、打开你的心智，让你能投资于生活的其他领域。

实现目标的具体"怎么做"暂时还不需要出现。仅仅设定目标，就能让你的心智去识别和学习"怎么做"。

接下来，你需要问自己一个问题：

你刚刚设定的目标和时间线，对现在的你来说是不可能的吗？换句话说，你当前的做法能把你带到那里吗？

如果能，这个目标就是线性的，而不是不可能的。否决它，再把它往上提。

目标必须位于你现有的技能、习惯和对可能性的假设之外，因为这恰恰是创造出一个向你展示新现实、而不是一张待办清单的透镜的东西。

最后，目标必须是单一的、自己选择的。

你不能有相互竞争的目标。那会分散专注，根本不会形成一个视角。

而且它不能继承自你的父母、你的文化、你的行业或你的算法。

设定这个目标的完成状态是：你能带着彻底的确信说"我就做这个"，并且你能立刻看出，你当前做的大部分事情都与它毫无关系。

欢迎来到未知。最有意义的所在。

2）为你不做的事设定标准，并坚守它们。

有了一个设定得当的妄想式目标，几乎没有什么现有的东西能帮你实现它。

所以，你现在必须审视你的生活。每一个习惯、项目、客户、关系、日常和义务。它们中有没有任何一个能帮你达成目标？答案几乎肯定是没有。

这是最难的部分。

你创建一个你拒绝降低的标准。

这些是你不再做的事，句号。

你不是"差不多"不再周末去派对，或者"差不多"不再给你的自由职业服务定低价，你是彻底消除它们。

当然，说起来容易做起来难。人们在这一点上挣扎，是因为沉没成本偏差、恐惧和缺乏问责。心智是一台自我欺骗的机器，而人类热爱对自己撒谎。

这是你练习残酷的自我诚实、一次撕掉好几块创可贴的机会。

提高标准的完成状态是：生活感觉大大简化了。你拥有更少的东西、更少的义务、更少的借口，它们不再堵塞你的心智、阻止你行动。

第 3 步）专注于你能走的杠杆最高的那条路。

你的目标创造了一个框架，让你能从中感知新的机会。

你的标准从你的生活中减去一切不服务于目标的东西，为你的注意力朝正确方向移动腾出空间。

现在你必须接受，真正能实现目标的路非常少。

但你不会发现一条完全成形的路、一个完全成形的人或机会。

对于健身这样的事，妄想式目标（不可能的结果 + 不可能的时间线）是 6 个月内参加一场体格比赛，或者今年第一次深蹲 405 磅。

当你设定这个目标时，看看你的注意力会发生什么。突然间，"去健身房、举铁、重复"的线性路径不管用了。你需要结构。你可能需要一位能让你快速走上正轨的教练。你当前的训练计划需要只专注于能把你带到那里的组数、次数和动作。你开始把睡眠和饮食看作严格的、不容商量的。所有这些都是你以前根本不会去想的东西，因为你之前的目标把它们当作噪音过滤掉了。

当我决定要达成我的第一个 10 万美元月收入时（对一个习惯了年收入不到 10 万美元、收入与时间绑定的自由职业者来说，这是一个妄想式目标），我在社交媒体上只有大约 1000 名粉丝。我显然不可能像其他所有人那样，靠"坚持发帖，希望算法注意到"到达那里。

我必须找到力量倍增器。我需要一个报价产品，所以我不再把我的帖子看作产品，而是开始把它们当作一样人们愿意付钱购买的东西的分发渠道。我需要比我那 1000 名粉丝更多的分发，所以我需要从一个拥有多得多粉丝的人那里借力，这意味着我必须去建立人脉，与一个拥有 5 万粉丝的人交换价值，让他们把我的帖子分享给他们的受众，从而为我的报价产品带来更多买家。

Tell me if any of these sound like you:

1) You feel like you’ve been lied to about what a good life looks like.

You’re pursuing a goal that sounds nice when you mention it at dinner with grandma. Or a goal that wouldn’t make your parents think you’re a disappointment. And you don’t see it going anywhere.

If you ignore that feeling, you wake up 10 years later with a degree, decent job, and a never ending sense of despair that you’ll never be able to chase your own dreams.

2) You’re productive, but not actually making progress toward anything.

You wake up early. You listen to podcasts. You journal, you lift, your calendar is full, but you don’t know where you’re going.

Give it 5 years and you’ll still be “figuring out what to do with your life.”

3) You’re ambitious, but stay quiet because it sounds embarrassing to your friends and family.

You want a unique life. You don’t want to fit in. But when people ask about your future, you water it down so nobody laughs at it.

You get stuck performing mental masturbation. You feel special for wanting to be unique, but you still do the same thing as everyone else.

Nobody wants to end up in any of those 3 situations.

This letter will change that for you.

Because one of my favorite things to do is study the actions of both successful and mediocre people. And that’s all success really is. Doing more of what leads to success and less of what leads to mediocrity. The next few letters will be dedicated to just that.

When we reverse engineer the people who have done the impossible - Elon Musk, Steve Jobs, and any of the other greatest creatives, thinkers, and builders of the past - there is one pattern I’ve noticed between them all.

Their goals are delusional.

That’s the key to their success.

And that’s exactly what we’re going to go over - why average goals lead to failure, the psychology of delusional goals, how to enter the same reality as ultra successful people, and how to achieve what most people think is impossible.

Why “realistic” goals lead to failure and mediocrity (you fall into this category by default)

The most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize a thing that should not exist. – Elon Musk

You have goals, even if you didn’t set them.

That’s how the mind works. And if you become aware of this, you can quickly see why you aren’t able to stick to any of the goals you set.

We’re not talking about New Years Resolutions or some other surface level goal you set and immediately quit because you felt bad about how you looked in the mirror and forgot about it the next day (because another unconscious goal (usually convenience and comfort, or the goal of not drawing attention to yourself) was more important to you than finally getting in shape).

We’re talking about how every thought you have or action you take is aiming toward a goal. Like a heat-seeking missile. You are silently optimizing for a goal you never chose, or someone else chose for you.

Take a second and think about that.

Every single thing you think or do is toward a goal.

That’s important. Extremely important. It means that if you don’t know what those goals are, you are not in control of your life.

Here’s how it goes:

• You are born

• Your first goal is survival

• Your parents have been thinking about what goals they want you to pursue for quite some time now. They “want the best for you”

• Your parents teach you how to walk, how to communicate, and eventually what their version of success for you looks like, which is usually the goals that society passed down to them (go to school, get a good job, praise your god)

• You go to school, then college because that’s just “what’s next”

• You get the job, you hit the milestones, you check the boxes

And then, after almost a quarter of your life, you start to wonder if this was even what you wanted in the first place.

Entropy illustrates that systems trend toward disorder. Applied to the mind, psychic entropy means that your mind, and therefore your life, drifts toward disorder and chaos. At some point, a system becomes unstable and a new, more ordered structure must emerge, like how a faucet spurting out water eventually turns into a whirlpool. In your life, you hit a point where the lower part of you wants to stay the same and the higher part wants something more. It feels like you’re being ripped in half. That is a signal to completely turn your life around.

The reason realistic goals lead to failure and mediocrity is because of how you choose them. You look at what you think is possible, then water it down a bit more so that people won’t laugh or think you’re an idiot. A goal only feels realistic if it fits the life your parents, teachers, or five closest friends already expected you to have.

In other words, your mind is in a cage.

And if you keep choosing to live in that cage, you’ll never have the chance to notice a potentially life-changing opportunity, let alone act on it, and your life slowly gets worse until the pain is so great you either get crushed or decide to pursue a delusional goal anyway.

The psychology of delusional goals

The man who conceives himself to be a “failure-type person” will find some way to fail, in spite of all his good intentions, or his willpower, even if the opportunity is literally dumped in his lap. The person who conceives himself to be a victim of injustice, one “who was meant to suffer,” will invariably find circumstances to verify his opinions. – Maxwell Maltz

Humans aren’t unique because they have memory or consciousness or a more refined intelligence than animals.

They’re unique because they constantly simulate the future to steer the present.

You think more about the future than the past, and the future is what causes your present behavior. Not the reverse. Your actions do not create the future. You create the future first, then act toward it.

It’s called selective attention.

Your brain either gets pulled by stimuli or directed by a goal.

And when you focus on one thing, that means, by definition, you’re not focused on the 99.99% of other things you could be focusing on. Your attention is goal directed rather than tossed around like a rag doll looking for the next cheap dopamine hit.

Delusional goals force this to happen.

Imagine two people with drastically different goals.

Tommy has the same goal as almost everyone else - go to school, get an education, get a decent job so he can finally afford a nice apartment, and acquire enough material status symbols to attract a mate.

Johnny has a goal that seems impossible - build a content business that makes $100k a month in 6 months.

Will Tommy ever have the possibility of making more money than what a “decent job” would pay him? Will Tommy ever learn something outside of school that allows him to pursue a more lucrative opportunity? No, because that’s not what his attention is selecting for. He could come across life changing information and his mind will consider it “noise” just because it’s not necessary to achieve the goal he’s pursuing.

Johnny, on the other hand, cannot think like the average person. To achieve that delusional goal, he realizes that most actions he’s taking are completely useless.

A properly set delusional goal pushes your brain into the unknown, accelerating neuroplasticity. Consistency reinforces pathways in your brain, but novelty and challenge stimulate learning and change faster. Flipping the switch and going all in on a goal throws your brain into an environment where it has no choice but to rewire, fast, and the new behavior becomes your new baseline.

That’s when flow state and obsession start to create a blissful quality of life. 5 intrinsic drivers start to stack.

• Curiosity – The desire to explore the unknown, learn how to change, and fill knowledge gaps. Results in good dopamine from novelty and norepinephrine, which heightens attention, preparing you to learn.

• Passion – An intense enthusiasm is built for the path that allows you to change your life. Results in more good dopamine and norepinephrine.

• Purpose – The feeling that your actions contribute to something larger than yourself. Achieving goals results in more dopamine which reinforces behavior. Serotonin stems from significance and belonging. Oxytocin stems from connection.

• Autonomy – The desire to direct your own life and work. To control your choices, actions, and environment. Results in yet again more dopamine and a reduction in cortisol (the stress from feeling put in a box), allowing for creative decision making.

• Mastery – The process of learning and growing is its own reward. Results in sustainable good dopamine that keeps you in the game.

This all sounds great, but I can hear your objection.

“But Dan, I’ve tried setting a goal like this before, but none of this happens.”

Yes.

Your goal needs gravity. It needs to be so strong that everything else in your life seems stupid and pointless.

That’s difficult to do, and I’ll be writing another letter about the intricacies of how to become obsessed with a life-changing goal, but for now let’s learn how to properly set a delusional goal.

How to enter the same reality of ultra successful people

Every great idea starts out as blasphemy. – Anthony De Mello

Steve Jobs would walk into a room, state an impossible deadline or an impossible product as if it were already decided.

“We’ll ship it by January, and it will do this” with such total conviction that the room of engineers who knew it was impossible would leave believing it, and then build toward it like it was simply the plan.

This was Job’s Reality Distortion Field.

Most people dismiss it as charisma, or manipulation, or him being an asshole, when it was simply him re-aiming the room’s attention.

Jobs refused to be pulled by the stimuli of “this timeline can’t possibly work,” and overrode it with a future target. Every brain in the room started selecting for a path instead of selecting for reasons it won’t work.

And that’s the thing.

Your goal is not a destination you hit. That’s one of the greatest self-help-esque interpretations of a goal there is. Instead, a goal is a filter that decides what your brain sees as important and what it ignores. A goal is a point of view. Whether you actually hit the goal or not is irrelevant because you will at least land near the goal, making progress you never thought possible before.

To properly set a delusional goal and create your own reality distortion field, here’s what needs to happen.

Pull out a notebook, write these down:

Step 1) Install the point of view.

First, think of a goal so big you think it’s impossible.

Then, set a timeline so short you think it’s impossible.

If you don’t know what goal to set, or even what to do with your life, it helps to think of the most painful problem in your life right now that makes other problems easier to solve.

For most people, that’s usually their fitness or finances.

Getting in the gym and dropping 20 pounds can do wonders for your confidence and energy, making it easier to start turning your life around.

Making more money relieves stress, opens your mind, and lets you invest in other areas of your life.

The specific “how” for achieving the goal doesn’t need to happen yet. Simply setting the goal lets your mind identify and learn the how.

Next, you need to ask yourself one question:

Is the goal and timeline you just set impossible for who you are now? In other words, can your current approach get you there?

If yes, the goal is linear, not impossible. Reject it and raise it further.

The goal must sit beyond your existing skills, habits, and assumptions of what’s possible because that’s exactly what creates a lens that shows you a new reality instead of a to-do list.

Last, the goal must be singular and self-chosen.

You cannot have competing goals. That splits focus and doesn’t lead to a point of view at all.

And it cannot be inherited from your parents, your culture, your industry, or your algorithm.

The finished version of setting this goal is when you can say “I do this” with total conviction and you can instantly see that most of what you’re currently doing has nothing to do with it.

Welcome to the unknown. The most meaningful place to be.

2) Set standards for what you don’t do, and stick to them.

With a properly set delusional goal, almost nothing will work to achieve it.

So, you must now audit your life. Every habit, project, client, relationship, routine, and obligation. Do any of them help you reach the goal? The answer is almost certainly no.

This is the hardest part.

You create a standard that you refuse to lower.

These are things you no longer do, period.

You don’t “almost” stop partying on the weekends or undercharging for your freelance services, you completely eliminate them.

Of course, this is easier said than done. People struggle with this because of sunk cost bias, fear, and a lack of accountability. The mind is a self-deception machine and humans love lying to themselves.

This is your chance to practice brutal self-honesty and rip multiple band-aids off.

The finished version of raising your standards is that life feels dramatically simpler. You have fewer things, fewer obligations, and fewer justifications that clog your mind and prevent you from action.

Step 3) Focus on the highest leverage path available to you.

Your goal creates a frame from which you can perceive new opportunities.

Your standards subtract everything from your life that does not serve the goal, creating space for your attention to move in the right direction.

Now you must accept that there are very few real paths to achieve the goal.

But you don’t find the path, person, or opportunity fully formed.

For something like fitness, the delusional goal (impossible outcome + impossible timeline) is to compete in a physique show in 6 months, or squat 405 for the first time this year.

When you set this, watch what happens to your attention. Suddenly the linear path of “show up, lift, repeat” doesn’t work anymore. You need structure. You may need a coach who can get you on the right track fast. Your current training program needs to solely focus on the sets, reps, and exercises that will get you there. You start seeing sleep and food as strict and non-negotiable. All things that you didn’t even think about because your previous goal filtered it out as noise.

When I decided I wanted to hit my first $100k month (delusional goal for a person who was used to making less than $100k a year as a freelancer who’s money was bound to time), I only had around 1000 followers on social media. I obviously wasn’t going to get there by “posting consistently and hoping the algorithm notices” like everyone else.

I had to find force multipliers. I needed an offer so I stopped seeing my posts as the product and started treating them as the distribution channel for one thing people pay for. I needed more distribution than my 1000 followers, so I needed to borrow from someone else who had a lot more followers, meaning I had to network and exchange value with someone who had 50,000 followers so they would share my posts to their audience, leading to more buyers of my offer.

Eden is second brain where you save any idea, research what ideas are already working, create content people can’t ignore, and soon with the next update... get paid for your knowledge (I am on a mission to make it frictionless to get your first product up, so you can start earning from your interests). At minimum, sign up for a free account and take a look around so you’re notified when that update goes live.

Now that your focus is singular, you are more “disciplined” than 99% of humanity, but you love it. It is one of the greatest things you can experience on this planet.

If you do not feel this joy, that is another problem.

In the next letter, we’ll talk about how to add gravity to your goals. That is, make your goals so meaningful that you can’t help but achieve them.

– Dan
