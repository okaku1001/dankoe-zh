The deep work routine that changed my life (1 hour every morning)

你每天能花 8 小时替别人造梦，就一定能腾出 1 小时造自己的梦。

但那一小时怎么用，决定了一切。

如果你每天一早头脑最清醒、干扰最少的时候，拿出 1 小时，只做那件最能推动你靠近理想生活的事，坚持 6 到 12 个月——你就有可能投入 365 小时在自己想要的人生上。这么多时间，能做的事远比你以为的多。

我这辈子大部分时间，每天只工作 2 到 4 小时。

网红炫耀身家总让人倒胃口。但为了说明结果，我也炫一下：正是每天这 2 到 4 小时，让我在全平台攒下将近 500 万粉丝；光靠个人品牌就赚了大约 1000 万美元，还没算我其他公司。那些时间，我用来发帖子、写文章、做 newsletter、拍 YouTube 视频、录播客。两本书也是这么写出来的，还有两本在路上——没用 AI 代笔。

我上班的时候，也没有每天 12 小时去做副业。只有 1 到 2 小时，周末再多一点。

如果你只想要那个简单到不能再简单的深度工作法，直接跳到这封信最后一节。

但如果你专注力比金鱼强一点，也明白你苦寻的「可操作建议」脱离语境就毫无意义，我建议你每一节都读。时间紧就先收藏，设个提醒回头再看。

你当然可以直接翻到后面几节。但如果你专注力比金鱼强一点，我还是建议每节都读。时间紧就收藏起来，设个提醒回头看。

我们来聊聊：为什么越忙的人越在底层；为什么去做人生使命以外的事都是浪费时间——尤其是在工作先慢慢消失、然后突然整体崩塌的时候；最后，怎么安排一天，不只是为了最高产出，而是为了最大杠杆。

慢慢看。

一、人类历史上大部分时候，越忙的人地位越低

亚里士多德说，我们工作是为了拥有闲暇；没有闲暇，没人能变得有智慧。

塞涅卡说，最忙的人往往最少成就，因为他们把日子花在了自己人生以外的一切上。

闲暇曾经是身份的象征，忙碌则是义务的标志。忙碌属于奴隶、农奴和从日出干到日落的劳工。

上个世纪的某个时候，这件事反过来了。

「我好忙」成了荣誉勋章，哪怕大多数人心知肚明：自己做的没什么意义，除了把时间投进别人的愿景，什么也没留下。

我相信它还会再翻一次。

很快，整天黏在电脑前、跟 Claude 来回发消息，会被看作（比现在更明显的）低身份的事。我们都有远大的梦想和抱负，但要把它们实现，靠的不是把每天 12 小时「苦干」当主要策略，而是一定程度的创造力。

能拿来思考、谋划和创造的时间，会成为高身份的标志。很多时候，它将是有钱人的专属。

刚才说每天工作 2 到 4 小时，其实有点撒谎。

作为写作者，如果把我散步、阅读、学习、思考、甚至睡觉的时间都算上……我其实一天「工作」24 小时。因为这些活动都在给写作的质量添柴。每天写 1 小时，就能触达几百万人；只要其中一小部分人向我或我的生意买单，我就能赚到几百万美元。有些人想不通这点，因为他们的收入和时间投入的劳动绑在一起。他们不是靠头脑的创造力赚钱——而这，恰恰是未来越来越必需的能力。

二、自己干才是历史常态，打工只是一段短暂的故障

专业化说到底只是一种包装漂亮的奴役。「专家」被哄着接受自己的奴役，因为他觉得换来的是一个在社会和文化上更受青睐、因此高度安全、可以干一辈子的位置。

——巴克敏斯特·富勒

工业化时期，一切都变成了工厂。

学校也不例外。

学校制度的唯一目的，就是让孩子为那个时代的岗位做好准备；而那个时代的岗位，是工厂岗位。我不禁好奇：当今天的工作剧变、甚至变得毫无意义时，教育会变成什么样？我相信我们正走向第二次文艺复兴，但这个以后再聊。

铃声标记一天的开始和结束。孩子按年龄分组，像一批批货。科目被拆开，像流水线。分数和考试是质检。学生因为听话而受奖励——按别人的时间表，朝别人的目标走。

读到这儿的人，大概都经历过这套系统。

心智是在长期规训中被塑造的，而我们的身份会牢牢抓住那些让我们有安全感、舒适感的东西。于是我们都在某种程度上，无意识地绑在了一种本来只是实验的工作方式上。

现代的「工作」概念只有一两百年历史。智人已经存在 30 万年。朝九晚五、拿工资、把自我价值挂在职位上——这不正常，也绝对不符合我们大脑的构造。生活很快变得机械。

从前，「工作」意味着拥有自己的工作。

1800 年，大多数美国人是农民、农场工人，或者经营家族生意。直到 18 世纪末，「job」这个词还只指「一件活儿」，不是你人生的 40 多年。

文艺复兴的理想是「全才」——很多人都能做得很好。工匠在行会里从学徒、熟练工一路做到大师。「杰作」最初的意思，是你为了证明自己已成大师而做出的那件作品。

我相信历史总在重演，我们正在进入工匠时代。工匠的价值，取决于他怎么用工具。今天，一台笔记本、互联网和 AI 都是任何人都能拥有的便宜工具。真正拉开差距的，是你追求的手艺、你对作品的愿景、你对细节的偏执。任何人都能做出任何东西，但注意力是有限的。所以只有那些把自己的事当成人生的使命、把全部身心投进去的人，才会脱颖而出。

阿尔文·托夫勒在《第三次浪潮》里提出过类似的看法。

他称之为「电子小屋」，把它看作工作的第三次浪潮。

第一次浪潮是农业，人们大多自己做自己用的东西。第二次浪潮是工厂，把人分成了生产者和消费者。第三次浪潮是信息与计算机，工作重新回到家里。我们再一次成为创造者。

他认为，过去很多工作都是「家庭手工业」：一家人在家纺纱、织布、做东西。工厂把工作从家里抽出来，塞进一栋大楼。他预言计算机会逆转这个过程，把工作重新带回家里（顺带一提，我此刻 10:09 坐在办公室里，穿着内裤写这些）。

——在第一次浪潮中，大多数人消费的是自己生产的东西。他们既不是通常意义上的生产者，也不是消费者，而是可以叫作「生产消费者」（prosumer）的人。

我认为这就是「创作者」的本义。未必是内容创作者——尽管在今天，创作者会在社交媒体上找到自己的受众，直到社交媒体被另一种媒介取代。

我有个网友卖纯棉衣服，因为他反对聚酯纤维。我的衣服就从他那儿买。另一个网友教哲学，我就从他那儿受教育。还有人卖软件，还有人卖烘焙食品并负责配送。诸如此类。我可以追求自己的兴趣，在一个数字部落里找到自己最能贡献的切入点——我们管这叫「利基」。创作者经济起起落落，但说到底，我把它看成一个数字社会：我们在里面学习、赚钱、创造，也为彼此的生活做贡献。这就是现代的文艺复兴式生活。

马歇尔·麦克卢汉把它称为「地球村」：我们这个高度专业化、碎裂化的文明，开始重新部落化。

如果你想按自己的兴趣活、按自己的方式过这一生，并且变得足够有适应力，不再担心失业，那么：

三、求你了，全身心投入你的人生使命

这颗星球上有将近 70 亿人。我希望有一天，也会有将近 70 亿家公司。

——纳瓦尔·拉维坎特

如果你关注科技和 AI，大概听过类似的话：

软件死了。信息死了。公司死了。一切都死了。每个人都会自己造软件，用 AI 拿到自己需要的信息。

那么，当整个过程让你觉得连开始都不值得时，你该怎么办？

第一，一切并没死。社交媒体特别擅长让人安于现状。

连 Jake Paul 都转去做信息产品了——去看看他的商业 YouTube 频道。说真的，那仍然是创作者最好的变现方式，哪怕 AI 已经掌握所有信息。（提示：如果一个人不知道该问 AI 什么，也不知道什么才算好建议，那他就拿不到自己需要的信息。对那些认同某种观点的人来说，人会充当教育和信息的中介。）

第二，考虑到很多人连饭都不自己做，还愿意给 Uber Eats 付高得离谱的钱，我非常怀疑他们真会去自己写软件。为了省下每月 10 到 20 美元的订阅费去造个工具，而不去做一件每小时能赚 1000 美元的杠杆动作，太蠢了。

再说，如果有一个人把「做出最好的内容排期 App」当成毕生事业，我宁愿付钱买他的产品，也不愿自己做（虽然我自己确实做了这样一个）。未来不会有 1000 个内容排期工具。我宁愿付钱给那个会把细节琢磨到极致的工匠，因为我自己去抠细节，只会让我偏离最重要的优先级。

「任何人都能做任何东西」，是我们这个时代最大的干扰。我们心里都清楚：长期专注在一个有意义、潜在回报高的目标上，才会带来最大的成果和最深的满足。未来不是人人都去做所有东西，而是人人做出自己的那一样，然后彼此交换。一个地球村。

既然如此，最好的做法就是：找到你的人生使命，用手边能用的工具把它做出来，再传播给那些认同你使命的人。

——所谓「人生使命」，是这样一种工作：它偏偏能让你把自己头脑的全部创造力释放出来，让你的潜力有个容器，也让你尽最大所能贡献于人类。

很难给「人生使命」贴一个精确标签。它也不需要你命名。很多人卡在「必须搞清楚自己到底在做什么」上。但这不是一份工作，也不是一串固定任务。它是一条通往未知的路。在这样的生活方式里，你的学习、思考和行动彼此协调，你用只有你自己的方式，为自己和世界做贡献。

这意味着，你选的事必须是真正深感兴趣的，而且几乎有无限上升空间。

换句话说，你大概得成为某种形式的创业者。

因为今天这种「工作」的技能天花板往往很低。你学到一定程度就可以躺着混了，工作变得重复、琐碎，你开始无聊，又没有自由蜕变成下一层的自己。等到那层壳也变得太窄，你就索性沉溺在那些会让人退化的事情里。

于是问题来了：

你怎么找到那件偏偏是你该做的事？

又怎么把它变成你的工作，让你既能体面地活下去，又能收获影响他人带来的意义感？

四、每天一小时。一个有意义的项目。一个关于未来的愿景。

代码和媒体是无需许可的杠杆。它们是新富背后的杠杆。你可以创造出在你睡觉时仍为你工作的软件和媒体。

——纳瓦尔·拉维坎特（又是他）

把这些串起来：

  • 不忙碌，是新的身份标志，也是真正创造性思考的必要条件。
  • 钟摆正在摆回「自己干、创业才是常态」。早点利用这一点。
  • 如果你想参与竞争，就必须做自己的人生使命——把自己交给一件事，避开干扰，不要什么都想造，专注质量和手艺。否则你就会被归到那堆生产垃圾内容的人里。

所有这些，指向同一件事的重要性：杠杆。

——杠杆，就是每小时努力换来更多结果，因为你停下来之后，有别的东西还在替你干活。

创造性思考、创业（工作的自由）和经验，是杠杆的必要前提。在「人人都能做任何事」的世界里，这是唯一的竞争方式。把时间投在人生使命以外的事情上，等于判自己死刑。

在一份工作里，你采取的雇员心态会把大脑训练成「反杠杆」的。大多数人的这种思维方式已经根深蒂固。

大多数人需要别人告诉自己该做什么。他们很难接受：任何事都能靠试错搞明白。我们今天这个世界就是这么来的；早在操作手册出现之前，人们真的做出过不可思议的事。

大多数人没法把时间和钱拆开。他们觉得一件活儿的价值是固定的：只要每周露面 40 小时，就能拿到工资。他们想象不到：只要只专注在杠杆最高的任务上，有可能用一小时赚到一整月的收入。

举个例子：我在 X 上发一篇文章，它不知怎么就攒到了惊人的 2.6 亿次浏览。其中 1% 的人访问产品页，再有 1% 的人花 150 美元下单——那就是 390 万美元。

但这是极端数字。

假如你持续发帖，做到每月几十万次曝光（其实没那么难：肯学、真去试、别两周就放弃——想把一件傻事练好，需要的时间不止两周）；并且你明白，可以不断调整帖子、落地页和报价，把转化率提到「还行」的 2.5%——那你赚的钱多到不知道怎么花。

这就是杠杆。

你手上有能带来流量的帖子，还有在你睡觉时仍在卖的产品。两者都与你的人生使命一致。

就像约会。如果你（产品）很有价值，却不把自己放到异性（流量）面前，那你约不到人。这很明显。如果你（产品）本身没价值，却把自己推到一大堆异性（流量）面前，你大概也约不到——至少约不到你想要的那种。

除了找到人生使命，真正要紧的就只有这两件事：改进你提供的东西——产品或服务；不断迭代你的帖子、广告、赞助，或任何你用来获取流量的手段。如果你既没有可投资的资本，也没有验证过的产品，最靠谱的路就是在社交媒体上做自然流量。

他们用内容来捕获注意力，因为注意力现在就在那里。你不喜欢社交媒体也没关系——靠报纸或电台做分发去建一家公司，祝你好运。

If you can spend 8 hours building someone else’s dream, you can spend 1 hour building your own.

But how you use that 1 hour determines everything.

If you were to focus on the one thing that leads to the most progress toward your ideal life, for 1 hour a day, first thing in the morning when your mind is clear and distractions are minimal, for 6-12 months, that’s 365 potential hours invested in the life you want. You can do far more than you think in that amount of time.

For most of my life, I worked 2-4 hours a day.

I feel like influencers flexing their millions put a sour taste in people’s mouth, but for the sake of results, I’ll flex a bit. Those 2-4 hours are exactly how I built an audience that’s closing in on 5 million followers across all platforms, and I’ve made just around $10m from my personal brand alone, not my other companies. That’s the time I used to put out posts, articles, newsletters, YouTube videos and podcasts. That’s also how I wrote 2 books with 2 more on the way - no AI writing involved.

And when I had a 9-5 job, I didn’t have 12 hours a day to make my side business a success. I had 1-2 hours, and a few more on the weekend.

Let’s discuss why busy people end up on the bottom, why pursuing anything but your life’s work is a waste of time - especially as jobs go extinct slowly then all at once, and finally, how to structure your day not only for maximum productivity, but for maximum leverage.

Enjoy.

I – For most of human history, the busiest people were the lowest status.

Aristotle said we work so that we can have leisure, and that without it no one becomes wise.

Seneca said that the busiest people were often the least accomplished, because they spent their days on everything except their own lives.

Leisure used to be a mark of status while busyness was a mark of obligation. Busyness belonged to slaves, serfs, and laborers who worked from sunrise to sundown.

At some point in the last century, that flipped.

“I’m so busy” became a badge of honor, even when most people knew they were working on nothing meaningful, with nothing to show except time invested in someone else’s vision.

I believe that this flip will happen again.

Soon, it will be seen (more than it already is) as low status to be glued to your computer, sending messages back and forth to Claude all day. We all have big dreams and aspirations, but it requires a certain level of creativity to achieve those dreams without 12 hours of “hard work” as your main strategy.

Time for thinking, strategizing, and creativity will be the mark of high status. In many cases, it will be reserved for the rich.

Now, when I say I worked 2-4 hours a day above, that was somewhat of a lie.

As a writer, if you were to add up the time I spend walking, reading, learning, thinking, and even sleeping... I “work” 24 hours a day. Because all of those activities fuel the quality of my writing, and one hour of writing a day can reach millions of people, and if a fraction of those people purchase from me or my business, I can make millions of dollars. Some people can’t wrap their head around this, because their income is tied to their time spent doing labor. They do not earn from the creative ability of their mind, which will only become more and more of a requirement in the future.

This is mostly why we’re building Eden. It is where you grow your audience and build a digital business. It’s where you sell your products, save all your ideas and bookmarks, and turn those unique ideas into content. If that content gets traction, then you promote your work and get paid for it. There’s a ￼30 day sprint that walks you through all of this. It’s a measly $5.

II - The historical normal is to be self-employed, working a job was a temporary glitch.

Specialization is in fact only a fancy form of slavery wherein the ‘expert’ is fooled into accepting his slavery by making him feel that in return he is in a socially and culturally preferred, ergo, highly secure, lifelong position. – Buckminster Fuller

During Industrialization, everything became a factory.

Schools included.

The sole purpose of a school system is to prepare children for the jobs of its time, and the jobs at the time were factory jobs - and I can’t help but wonder how education is going to look when the jobs of our time drastically change, or just become meaningless. I believe we are heading into a Second Renaissance, but I’ll save that for later.

Bells marked the start and end of the day. Kids are grouped by age, like batches. Subjects are split up, like an assembly line. Grades and tests were quality checks. Students were rewarded for doing what they were told, on someone else’s schedule, toward someone else’s goal.

Everyone reading this likely went through this system.

And since the mind is shaped through conditioning, and our identity latches onto that which provides us security and comfort, we are all in some way unconsciously tied to a style of work that was only an experiment.

The modern concept of a job is only 100-200 years old. Homo sapiens are 300k years old. Working 9-5, being paid a wage, and having your worth attached to your title is not normal, and it definitely isn’t beneficial to how our brains are wired. Life quickly becomes mechanical.

Work used to mean owning your work.

In 1800, most Americans were farmers, farm workers, or ran a family business. The word “job” meant one piece of work until the late 1700s. It did not mean 40+ years of your life.

The Renaissance ideal was the “universal man” who did many things well. Craftsmen worked under a guild, progressing from apprentice to journeyman to master. “Masterpiece” first meant the work you made to prove you were a master.

I believe history is repeating itself, as it always does, and we are entering the era of the craftsman. A craftsman’s worth was determined by how they used their tools. Today a laptop, the internet, and AI are cheap tools anyone can own. The craft they pursue, their vision for their work, and obsession over the details is what makes the difference. Anyone can build anything, but attention is finite, so only those who treat their work as their life’s work, pouring their heart and soul into what they do will rise above.

In The Third Wave, Alvin Toffler had a similar theory.

He called it the Electronic Cottage, which he saw as the third wave of work.

The first wave was farming, people mostly made what they used. The second wave was the factory, which split people into producers and consumers. The third wave is information and computers, where work moves back home. We once again become creators.

His argument is that a lot of work was “cottage industry,” where families spun, wove, and made goods at home. The factory pulled work out of the home and into one big building. He predicted that computers would reverse that and bring work home again (I’m sitting in my office, in my underwear, at 10:09 am, writing this, by the way).

During the First Wave most people consumed what they themselves produced. They were neither producers nor consumers in the usual sense. They were instead what might be called prosumers.

I believe that’s what a creator is. Not a content creator per se, although social media is where a creator would find their audience in today’s world, until social media is replaced with another form of media.

A friend I have online sells cotton apparel because he is against polyester clothing. I can get my apparel from him. Another online friend teaches philosophy, I can get my education from him. Another sells software. Another sells and ships baked goods. So on and so forth. I can pursue my interests and find a wedge in a digital tribe that I can contribute best to - we call this a “niche.” The creator economy has had its ups and downs, but at the end of the day, I see it as a digital society where we can learn, earn, create, and contribute to each other’s lives. That is the modern renaissance lifestyle.

Marshal McLuhan called this the global village where our specialist and fragmented civilization begins to retribalize.

If you have any desire to pursue your interests, do what you want with your life, and become adaptable enough to not have to worry about job loss:

III – I’m begging you to go all in on your life’s work

There are almost 7B people on this planet. Someday, I hope, there will be almost 7B companies. – Naval Ravikant

If you are up to date with tech and AI, you’ve probably heard something like this:

Software is dead. Info is dead. Companies are dead. Everything is dead. Everyone is just going to build their own software and get the information they need with AI.

So what do you do when it feels worthless to even start?

First, everything is not dead. Social media is great at keeping you complacent.

Jake Paul, of all people, has pivoted to the info space - look at his business YouTube channel, because frankly they are still the best way for creators to monetize, even when AI has all information (hint: if someone doesn’t know what to ask AI, and they don’t know what good advice looks like, then they won’t get the information they need - humans will be proxies for education and information for people who resonate with their point of view.)

Second, considering people don’t even cook their own food and pay far too much for Uber Eats, I highly doubt they will build their own software. Building a tool to replace a $10-20 subscription instead of focusing on one lever-moving action that makes $1000 an hour is foolish.

Plus, I would rather pay someone who’s made it their life’s work to create the best, say, content scheduling app than build one myself (although that’s what I built), there aren’t going to be 1000 content schedulers in the future. I would rather pay the craftsman who is going to obsess over the details, because obsessing over the details myself would just distract me from moving my own priorities forward.

“Anyone can build anything” is the greatest distraction of our time when we are all painfully aware that long-term focus on one meaningful goal with high potential payoff is what leads to the greatest result and most fulfillment. The future isn’t everyone building everything, but everyone building their one thing and trading with each other. A global village.

In that case, the best course of action is to discover what your life’s work is, and use the tools available to you to build it and spread it to an audience of people who resonate with your mission.

Your “life’s work” is work that allows you, specifically, to unleash the full creative ability of your mind, so that you have a vessel for your potential and contribute to humanity at your fullest.

It is difficult to label exactly what your life’s work is. It isn’t something you need to name. People get hung up on knowing exactly what they do as their life’s work. This isn’t a job. It’s not a set string of tasks. It’s a path in the unknown. It is a way of life where your learning, thinking, and acting are in harmony, and you are contributing to yourself and the world in a way only you can.

That means the work you choose must be something you have a deep interest in, and allows for near-infinite upward movement.

In other words, you’ll probably have to become an entrepreneur of some form.

Because with a “job,” as we define it today, there tends to be a low skill ceiling. You reach a point where you’ve learned enough to coast, the work becomes repetitive and mundane, and you get bored with no freedom to grow into your next shell, until even that shell becomes too constrained, so you overindulge in degenerative activities.

The question then is:

How do you find the thing that you, specifically, were meant to do?

And how do you turn that into your work so that you can comfortably survive and reap the meaning that comes from impacting others?

IV – One hour a day. One meaningful project. One vision for the future.

Code and media are permissionless leverage. They’re the leverage behind the newly rich. You can create software and media that works for you while you sleep. – Naval Ravikant (again)

To tie this all together:

– Non-busyness is the new mark of status, and it is required for true creative thinking

– The pendulum is swinging back to self-employment and entrepreneurship being the norm, take advantage early

– If you want to compete, you must pursue your life’s work - you must dedicate yourself to one thing, avoid distractions, not try to build everything, and focus on quality and craftsmanship - or else you will be bucketed in with everyone else producing slop

All of these things are arguing the importance of one thing - leverage.

Leverage is getting more of a result per hour of effort, because something other than your time keeps doing the work after you stop.

Creative thinking, entrepreneurship (freedom of work), and experience are necessary pre-requisites to leverage. It is the only way to compete in a world of ‘everyone can do anything.’ Committing to anything but your life’s work is a death sentence.

At a job, the employee mindset you adopt conditions your mind to be anti-leverage. And most people have this way of thinking deeply ingrained in them.

Most people need to be told what to do. They have difficulty accepting that anything can be figured out through the process of trial and error - that’s how the world as we know it came to be, and people did in fact do incredible things before there were instruction manuals for doing them.

Most people can’t dissociate time from money. They see a task’s worth as flat, because as long as they show up for 40 hours a week, they’ll get a paycheck. They cannot fathom that there is some way to make an entire months worth of their income in an hour, if they simply focused only on the tasks with the highest leverage.

As an example, I can publish an X article, and that article can somehow amass a freakish 260 million views, and 1% of those people visit a product page, and 1% of them purchase that product for $150, that’s $3.9 million.

But those are crazy numbers.

What if you post consistently, to the point of generating a few hundred thousand impressions a month (not that hard to do, if you learn and actually try and don’t quit after 2 weeks - it takes longer than 2 weeks to get good at something silly), and you understand that you can tweak the post, the landing page, and the offer to increase conversions to an “okay” 2.5%, then that’s more money than you’d know what to do with.

That’s leverage.

You have posts that generate traffic, and you have a product that sells while you sleep. Both are in alignment with your life’s work.

It’s like dating. If you (the product) are valuable, but you don’t put yourself in front of men/women (traffic), then you aren’t going to get a date. It’s obvious. If you (the product) aren’t valuable, and you put yourself in front of a ton of men/women (traffic) then you probably won’t get a date either, at least not one that you want.

Outside of finding what your life’s work is, those are quite literally the only things that matter. Improving what you offer - a product or service - and iterating on your posts, ads, sponsorships or whatever else you use to generate traffic. For someone who doesn’t have capital to invest or a proven offer, your best bet is organic posts on social media.

Translating this - what do you actually do?

Block out 1 hour a day. Work on 1 meaningful project. Aim for 1 vision for your future. Take it 1 day at a time, for 1 year. 1 hour of deep work is better than 0 hours, and you can drastically change your life in 365 hours.

Then, increase to 2 hours, maybe even 3. You’ll have other work to do as well, but I’m more worried about focusing on executing the highest priority, highest leverage task first thing in the morning to ensure it gets done.

Because when was the last time you asked yourself, before you started working, if I were to do one thing today, what would take me the furthest to my goal?

Probably never.

So what do you do during that one hour?

If you don’t have a product, build one during that hour.

Once you have the product, start writing content.

That’s how businesses are built right now.

Look at Jordan Peterson, Andrew Huberman, and the rest. They probably don’t register themselves as “content creators” or “personal brands.” They have a deep obsession with their interests, they share their learnings and worldview with the world in hopes of helping people.

They use content as the way to capture attention, because that’s where the attention is right now. It doesn’t matter if you don’t like social media - good luck building a business with the newspaper or radio as your distribution.
